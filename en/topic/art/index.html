The bull nobody ordered

Nobody ordered the bronze bull. The sculptor Arturo Di Modica cast it with his own money and, one night in December 1989, without any permission, trucked it in and set it down in front of the New York Stock Exchange. A Christmas present to the city. The bull weighs over three tonnes.

The police hauled it away, the public objected, and the bull was put up nearby, where it still stands.

Fearless Girl was installed in 2017, facing the bull. A year later she was moved to the Stock Exchange. Now she stares at the Exchange, and the bull stares at nothing.

Nineteen and thirty-eight

The Korean War Veterans Memorial has 19 steel soldiers. Beside them stands a polished granite wall that reflects them. That makes 38. It's deliberate: the 38th parallel is the line along which Korea was divided before the war, and roughly where the border runs today.

A museum opened in a crash

MoMA opened on 7 November 1929, nine days after the stock market crash that started the Great Depression. We were there almost exactly on its birthday: 6 November. Van Gogh's Starry Night has been in the museum since 1941.

How the lions got their names

At first the lions were named after the library's founders: Leo Astor and Leo Lenox, later Lord Astor and Lady Lenox. Their current names, Patience and Fortitude, came from Mayor Fiorello La Guardia in the 1930s, during the Great Depression. He thought those were the two qualities New Yorkers needed to get through it.

The theatre Disney saved

The New Amsterdam Theatre, home of Aladdin, opened in 1903. In the 1910s and 20s it hosted the famous Ziegfeld Follies. Later it became a cinema, fell into decay and stood closed for years. In the 1990s Disney restored it, and it's been a theatre again ever since.

The Ghostbusters firehouse

It isn't a set. The building at 14 North Moore Street in Tribeca went up in 1903 and has housed a working fire company, Hook & Ladder 8, ever since. The 1984 film used only the outside. The interior scenes were shot in Los Angeles, in an old fire station there.

In 2011 the city planned to close the company to save money. New Yorkers fought for it and won. The ghost sign that now hangs over the doors was made and donated by fans of the film from Buffalo.
