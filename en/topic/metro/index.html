Why we got confused

The New York subway opened in 1904. It has 472 stations, more than any other metro in the world, and it runs around the clock.

Visitors almost always get confused the same way: many lines have express and local trains. They follow the same route, sometimes from the same platform, but the express skips the small stations. Get on the wrong one and you sail past your stop. That's roughly what happened to us on the first evening.

No tokens, no cards

The famous New York tokens are long gone, and the yellow MetroCard is on its way out too. Now you pay with OMNY: tap a bank card or a phone on the turnstile and you're through. No ticket to buy at all.

A $150 dinner and our first subway

$150

dinner for three, tax and tip included

In the evening we had dinner at a café. The bill was $150. That's not a typo. Here they add tax to the menu price, add a tip to the tax, and add to the tip a feeling that you've done something wrong.

Makar fell asleep right at the table, deeply, like a grown-up. A taxi to the hotel would have been another sixty dollars or so. We looked at our sleeping son, then at each other, and went down into the subway.

In the subway we spent a long time working out which train we needed. Natasha carried Makar. I carried the responsibility. We figured it out in the end.

The subway broke, run for the bus

We left early in the morning. That morning the subway broke down. We started the trip unable to figure it out, and ended it with the subway unable to figure us out.

We ran for a bus. On the plus side, from its window we filmed the outskirts, not just the postcard centre. That's New York too; people just rarely look at it.
