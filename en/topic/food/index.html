Where the extra dollars come from

New York menu prices don't include tax. The bill adds 8.875% sales tax, and then you're expected to tip, usually 18–20% of the pre-tax amount. So $100 of food on the menu becomes about $130. Our $150 dinner was, roughly, a $115 menu.

The sign above the table

Katz's has been open since 1888. During the Second World War their slogan was "Send a salami to your boy in the army," and they really did ship salami to soldiers overseas.

Above one table hangs a sign: "Where Harry met Sally... hope you have what she had!" That's the table where the famous scene from the 1989 film was shot.

Two shades of pickle

While you wait for your sandwich at Katz's, a plate of pickles lands on the table. They come in two colours, and that's no accident.

The bright green ones are half-sours: they've had only a short stay in the brine and still crunch, almost like fresh cucumbers. The dark olive ones are full-sours, fermented all the way. Neither has any vinegar. Just salt, water, garlic, dill and time. In New York this is called a kosher dill, though it needn't be kosher in the religious sense: here "kosher" means "made the way the Jewish pickle men made it".

And they made it right here. Katz's is on the corner of Houston and Ludlow, and in the early 20th century Essex and Ludlow Streets were the "pickle district": barrels sold straight off the pavement, around eighty shops. Then immigration was restricted, pushcarts were banned, and nearly all of them closed. The last one from that era, Guss' Pickles, is still going.

The halal carts

Food carts stand on almost every New York corner, and half of them say "halal". The most famous, The Halal Guys, began in 1990 as an ordinary hot dog cart at 53rd Street and Sixth Avenue. Two years later the owners decided a hot dog wasn't a proper meal and switched to chicken and rice. Taxi drivers loved it first, and then the whole city.

Plus Deposit

Coca-Cola in a glass bottle, $2.29. Under the price, in small print: "plus deposit". Since 1982 New York State has charged a five-cent deposit on every bottle and can, refunded when you return the container. Hence the people outside supermarkets with enormous bags of cans.

Spider-Man's pizzeria

Joe's Pizza was opened in 1975 by Pino Pozzuoli, who came from Naples. Spider-Man 2 made it world-famous: Peter Parker works there as a delivery boy and gets fired for being late. On our last evening we went into Joe's on Broadway at 40th Street, by Times Square. Spider-Man is right there on the sign.

The pizza principle

Late in the evening we got pizza to the room. New Yorkers have a "pizza principle": since the early 1960s a slice cost the same as a subway ride. A New Yorker named Eric Bram noticed and described it in 1980, and it held with uncanny precision for decades. Recently, though, it broke: the slice is now the cheaper one.

A $150 dinner and our first subway

$150

dinner for three, tax and tip included

In the evening we had dinner at a café. The bill was $150. That's not a typo. Here they add tax to the menu price, add a tip to the tax, and add to the tip a feeling that you've done something wrong.

Makar fell asleep right at the table, deeply, like a grown-up. A taxi to the hotel would have been another sixty dollars or so. We looked at our sleeping son, then at each other, and went down into the subway.

In the subway we spent a long time working out which train we needed. Natasha carried Makar. I carried the responsibility. We figured it out in the end.
