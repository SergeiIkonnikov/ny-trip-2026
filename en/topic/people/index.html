SoHo gets dressed

In SoHo everyone is dressed as if they have a photo shoot in five minutes. Maybe they do.

The name is short for South of Houston Street. The urban planner Chester Rapkin coined it in 1962. Back then it was emptying factories in cast-iron buildings. Then artists moved into the huge cheap lofts, in 1971 they were officially allowed to live there, and galleries and boutiques followed the artists. Now the artists can't afford it.

The man in red

On Times Square everyone has a job. This man in the red jacket was selling tickets for the double-decker sightseeing bus. He was trying very hard.

At some point the city got tired of costumed characters and sellers working the tourists, and in 2016 it divided the square into zones. In some you can't stand still; in others you're allowed to work the crowd.

A wedding by the rock

In November everyone in Central Park is having their picture taken: newlyweds, models, someone's portfolio. In one hour we saw a wedding and a professional shoot with a flash and umbrella.

The grey rocks everyone poses against aren't scenery. They're outcrops of the bedrock all of Manhattan stands on. The park was designed around them.

The woman who was everywhere

All day the same woman kept walking into our shots. At the cemetery, in a red top. At the White House. On the pavement.

We don't know her. By the evening she was almost part of the group. If you recognise yourself, thank you: the day would have been duller without you.
