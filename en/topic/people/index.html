Nineteen and thirty-eight

The Korean War Veterans Memorial has 19 steel soldiers. Beside them stands a polished granite wall that reflects them. That makes 38. It's deliberate: the 38th parallel is the line along which Korea was divided before the war, and roughly where the border runs today.

How the lions got their names

At first the lions were named after the library's founders: Leo Astor and Leo Lenox, later Lord Astor and Lady Lenox. Their current names, Patience and Fortitude, came from Mayor Fiorello La Guardia in the 1930s, during the Great Depression. He thought those were the two qualities New Yorkers needed to get through it.

Where Hamilton was shot

The Weehawken waterfront, where we looked at Manhattan, is known for something else too. Here, on the bank of the Hudson, in 1804, Alexander Hamilton, one of America's founding fathers, fought a duel with Vice President Aaron Burr. Hamilton was fatally wounded. His face is on the ten-dollar bill now, and there's a Broadway musical about him, Hamilton.

The woman who finished the bridge

The Brooklyn Bridge was designed by John Roebling, who died before construction even began. His son Washington took over, and then he too was struck down by caisson disease from working under the river. From then on he watched the construction through a telescope from his bedroom.

Every instruction to the site went through his wife, Emily Roebling. She taught herself the maths, the strength of materials and bridge engineering, and for eleven years effectively ran the project. When the bridge opened in 1883 she was the first to ride across it, holding a rooster, a symbol of victory.

The teacher in the barrel

The first person to go over Niagara Falls in a barrel and survive was a schoolteacher, Annie Edson Taylor. It was 1901, on her 63rd birthday. She hoped for fame and fortune. She got the fame, not the fortune. Climbing out of the barrel, she said nobody should ever try it again.

The guard that never leaves

At the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier at Arlington, an honour guard has stood without a break since 1937: day and night, through heat, snow and hurricanes. The sentinel takes exactly 21 steps along the tomb, turns, waits 21 seconds and walks back. 21, like the 21-gun salute, the highest military honour.

A $150 dinner and our first subway

$150

dinner for three, tax and tip included

In the evening we had dinner at a café. The bill was $150. That's not a typo. Here they add tax to the menu price, add a tip to the tax, and add to the tip a feeling that you've done something wrong.

Makar fell asleep right at the table, deeply, like a grown-up. A taxi to the hotel would have been another sixty dollars or so. We looked at our sleeping son, then at each other, and went down into the subway.

In the subway we spent a long time working out which train we needed. Natasha carried Makar. I carried the responsibility. We figured it out in the end.
